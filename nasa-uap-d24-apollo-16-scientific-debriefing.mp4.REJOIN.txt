This is a raw byte split, not a media-level segmentation. The concatenation is
bit-identical to the file served by DVIDS — no container rewrap, no re-encode.
The individual parts are deliberately NOT playable on their own; that is the cost
of keeping the archived bytes exact.

Why split: GitHub LFS enforces a hard 2 GiB per-object ceiling. The whole file was
downloaded and verified, then rejected on push:

    [6802ea02...] Size must be less than or equal to 2147483648: [422]

Splitting was chosen over the alternatives because it is the only one that keeps the
government's exact bytes inside this repository:

  - Transcoding under 2 GiB was rejected. The video track is genuine 1080p footage at
    4319 kb/s, not a static slate over an audio record, so re-encoding would discard
    real archival content.
  - Time-based segmentation with `ffmpeg -c copy` would give two playable MP4s, but the
    container rewrap means the pieces no longer concatenate to the original bytes.
  - Hosting the single file off GitHub, or leaving a pointer to the CloudFront URL,
    would defeat the point of the mirror for exactly the record most worth mirroring:
    the corpus exists so the bytes survive independently of the government's host.
